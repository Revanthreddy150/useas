Final Project Submission and Evaluation

Evaluation Problem Prompt & Given Metrics:
Comprehensive Evaluation Submission:
The model's performance metrics indicate a good fit on training data but poor generalization. A Training R² of 0.91 shows the model explains 91% variance in training data, while Test R² of 0.68 shows a significant drop of 23 points, which is a classic sign of overfitting. The model has memorized training patterns rather than learning generalizable relationships. The MSE (Test) of 28,500 is relatively high, indicating large average prediction errors on unseen houses. The residual pattern of "increasing spread with higher predicted prices" indicates heteroscedasticity, meaning the model's errors are not random and its accuracy decreases for expensive houses, violating linear regression assumptions. Two potential data issues revealed are: First, overfitting due to high model complexity or too many features relative to data size, causing the model to capture noise. Second, heteroscedasticity and non-linearity; house price variance naturally increases with size and grade, and features like waterfront view may have non-linear impact that a simple linear model cannot capture. Also, there may be outliers in high-priced homes and skewed price distribution. Three improvement strategies: 1) Regularization (Ridge/Lasso): Apply Ridge regression with tuned alpha via cross-validation. This shrinks coefficients and penalizes complexity, reducing the gap between train and test R² and addressing overfitting. 2) Feature Engineering and Transformation: Log-transform the target variable (price) and apply PolynomialFeatures for square footage and grade to capture non-linear relationships, and use StandardScaler for normalization. This will stabilize variance and fix heteroscedasticity. 3) Cross-validation and Robust Evaluation: Implement train_test_split and cross_val_score with K-fold to better estimate performance, and use residual plots and GridSearchCV to tune hyperparameters, ensuring the model is evaluated on unseen data before deployment. In real-world data analysis, model refinement is iterative. Initial high training scores can be misleading; true success is measured by consistent test performance and well-behaved residuals, requiring continuous evaluation, transformation, and regularization to build a reliable business model.
